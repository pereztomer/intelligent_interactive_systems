# Theory

Our Presentation Rehearsal Coach is grounded in foundational theories of Human-AI Interaction, addressing \citet{grosz2012}'s central question: "How can humans and AI systems think well together?" The system's design is informed by multiple theoretical frameworks that guide both its architecture and interaction patterns.

## Human-AI Collaboration Framework

\citet{grosz2012} poses the fundamental question of how humans and AI systems can effectively collaborate, emphasizing that AI should enhance rather than replace human capabilities. Our system directly addresses this question through a hybrid architecture that combines algorithmic precision (signal processing) with AI interpretation (LLM orchestration). This design exemplifies human-AI collaboration: users practice presentations using their expertise and judgment, while the AI provides expert-level feedback that would be difficult to obtain otherwise. Together, they achieve better outcomes than either could alone, with the system acting as an assistant-level collaborator that supports rather than replaces human expertise.

## Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction

\citet{amershi2019}'s framework provides 18 design guidelines for effective Human-AI Interaction, of which our system addresses multiple core principles. Most critically, the system implements **Guideline 11** (make clear why the system did what it did) by providing transparent explanations that connect raw metrics to presentation concepts. For instance, rather than merely reporting "15 filler words," the system explains that "high filler word usage combined with fast pacing suggests nervousness." The system also adheres to **Guideline 5** (show contextually relevant information) through multi-modal analysis that synthesizes audio, navigation, and content data to provide contextually appropriate feedback. Additionally, **Guideline 3** (time services based on context) is implemented by providing feedback exclusively after presentation attempts, never during active performance, respecting the user's presentation flow.

## AI-Mediated Communication Framework

\citet{hancock2020} define AI-Mediated Communication (AI-MC) as "interpersonal communication in which an intelligent agent operates on behalf of a communicator by modifying, augmenting, or generating messages to accomplish communication goals." Our system fits this definition precisely: the AI operates on behalf of the user to enhance their presentation communication. The system's AI-MC dimensions include moderate magnitude (interpreting rather than generating content), multi-modal media type (audio + text + navigation), optimization for presentation quality, low autonomy (user controls when to analyze), and sender-focused role orientation (enhancing the user's presentation). This framework informed our design by emphasizing the importance of maintaining user agency and ensuring the AI serves the user's communication goals rather than replacing their voice.

## Interpretability and Explanation Design

Two complementary theories guide how our system presents feedback. \citet{lipton2016} identifies four key reasons for interpretability: trust, causality, transferability, and informativeness. Our system addresses these by showing both objective metrics and AI interpretation, enabling users to verify reasoning and build appropriate trust. The system prioritizes informativeness—the real purpose is to aid users in making decisions about their presentations, so explanations carry extra value beyond mere predictions.

\citet{miller2017} draws from social sciences to provide design principles for explanations, emphasizing that people prefer contrastive, simple, and conversational explanations. Our system implements these principles by providing contrastive feedback (e.g., "Your pacing improved compared to attempt 1"), keeping explanations simple and focused (2-3 key points), and using a conversational, supportive tone that aligns with the "expert presentation coach" role. These principles ensure feedback is not only accurate but also understandable and actionable.

## Algorithm-in-the-Loop Decision Making

\citet{green2019} examine how people make decisions when informed by algorithms, emphasizing the importance of reliability, fairness, and appropriate trust calibration. Our system implements this framework by providing AI feedback that supports rather than replaces user decision-making. Users make final decisions about their presentations; the AI provides information to inform those decisions. The system helps users calibrate their trust by showing both raw metrics and AI interpretation, enabling users to understand when to rely on AI feedback and when to apply their own judgment. This approach addresses \citet{green2019}'s finding that people often cannot reliably assess algorithm performance by providing transparency that supports better trust calibration.

Together, these theoretical foundations ensure our system provides transparent, contextually appropriate, and explainable feedback that supports users' presentation skill development while maintaining their agency and building appropriate trust in the AI system.
